Those stays look nothing alike, and neither should the way you book them. First, Match the Booking to the Stay

Most bad accommodation decisions come from booking the wrong type of place, not the wrong price. Start here:

One or two nights
A hotel near the centre or the station

For a single night before a conference or an early train, a hotel is hard to beat on convenience. You pay for the front desk and the breakfast, and you accept a room rather than a home.

Three nights to several weeks
A serviced apartment

The sweet spot for most visitors to Padova. A whole apartment with a kitchen, a washing machine and room to spread out, usually cheaper per night than two hotel rooms for a family, and far better suited to a stay where you'll want to cook at least sometimes.

One month and beyond
A furnished medium-term rental

For students, relocating professionals, visiting academics and patients undergoing treatment. Furnished, bills included, monthly rate, proper contract, no year-long lease. Almost always the cheapest way to spend a month or more in the city.

Where to Stay in Padova, Area by Area

The historic centre

Between Piazza delle Erbe, Piazza dei Signori and the Palazzo della Ragione you are inside the part of Padova people travel to see. Markets under the arcades every morning, restaurants at every turn, everything walkable. The trade-off is that parking is restricted and streets can be lively at night.

Around Prato della Valle and the Basilica

Quieter, extremely beautiful, and the right base if you're here for the Basilica of Saint Anthony or the Botanical Garden. Still an easy walk into the centre.

Near the railway station

The practical choice if you're commuting to Venice, Vicenza or Verona, or arriving late. Trains to Venice take well under an hour, which is why a good number of people now stay in Padova and day-trip to Venice rather than the reverse.

The university quarter and Portello

Student energy, cheap food, canal-side bars in summer. Convenient for anyone with business at UniPD or the university hospital.

Toward the hospital and the fair district

Less scenic, more practical. If your stay is built around treatment at the Azienda Ospedale or an event at the Fiera, being ten minutes away on foot matters more than a view.

Then Choose How You Book — This Is Where the Money Goes

The same apartment, on the same dates, can cost noticeably different amounts depending on where you click "reserve". The reason is structural rather than sneaky: the large booking platforms charge the property a commission, and most of them also add a service fee on the guest's side at checkout. Both of those have to come from somewhere, and they end up inside the nightly rate you're quoted.

Large booking platforms
Wide choice and a familiar interface. But the property pays a commission on every reservation, and you're typically charged a guest service fee on top. Messages go through the platform, and the terms are the platform's, not the host's.
Booking direct
No intermediary commission and no platform service fee, so the same night can be cheaper. You talk to the people who actually manage the apartment, and any flexibility on check-in, length of stay or a late arrival can be agreed with them directly.

A Few Practical Things to Know

  • City tax. Like every Italian city, Padova applies a tourist tax per person per night for stays in tourist accommodation, with the usual exemptions for children and certain categories. We tell you the exact amount and how to pay it before you arrive, so it's never a surprise at the door.
  • Registration is required by law. Every guest's ID has to be registered with the authorities on arrival — this is standard across Italy and applies to hotels and apartments alike.
  • Parking needs planning. Much of the centre is a restricted traffic zone. Ask us before you arrive with a car; each apartment has its own answer.
  • Book early for the busy weeks. Graduation periods, the Saint Anthony feast in June, trade fair dates and the university term start all compress availability across the whole city.
Frequently Asked Questions
What is the best way to book accommodation in Padova?
Decide first on the type of stay — a hotel for a night, a serviced apartment for a few days to a few weeks, a furnished medium-term rental for a month or more — then book with the property directly wherever you can. Is it cheaper to book direct than through a large booking platform?
Usually, yes. Platforms charge the property a commission on every reservation and most add a guest service fee at checkout, and both are ultimately reflected in the price you see. Booking through a property's own site removes those layers. It also means the terms, the flexibility and any special request are agreed with the people who actually manage the apartment.
Where is the best area to stay in Padova?
It depends on why you're coming.
